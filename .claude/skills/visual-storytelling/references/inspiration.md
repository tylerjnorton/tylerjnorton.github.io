# Inspiration Catalog

Award winners crawled from the Information is Beautiful Awards 2024 showcase and the
World Data Visualization Prize 2019 gallery. Each entry lists the live URL and the
specific technique it proves — steal the technique, not the surface style.

Use this file two ways: (1) before choosing a form, find the entry whose *story shape*
matches yours and start from its pattern; (2) when a draft feels generic, find the rule
it's breaking here and look at how the winner solved it.

## Narrative / scrollytelling

### This is a Teenager — The Pudding (Gold, 2024)
https://pudding.cool/2024/03/teenagers/
Unit visualization as biography. Opens on one named 13-year-old in 1997, then scrolling
advances time and widens to hundreds of individual marks, each a real study participant.
Layers accumulate per step (GPA → college → income → health), so consequences compound
visually the way they do in life. Key stats anchored to lived scale ("the US poverty
line is about $15,000"). **Proves:** unit-viz narrative, specific-then-general opening,
one-message-per-step pacing.

### Is the Love Song Dying? — The Pudding (Gold + Most Beautiful, 2024)
https://pudding.cool/2024/11/love-songs/
A question headline the whole internet wants answered. Builds a color-coded taxonomy of
love-song types, then runs it across six decades of Billboard Top 10 as a flowing
stacked composition. Song titles float as data points; you can hear snippets. Playful
palette earned by a playful subject. **Proves:** question headlines, taxonomy-as-palette,
sound as a data channel, rigor under whimsy (methodology published).

### The world's hunger watchdog warned of catastrophe. Famine struck anyway. — Reuters (Gold, 2024)
https://www.reuters.com/graphics/FAMINE-RESPONSE/SUDAN/zdvxqkqxnpx/
Investigative data journalism: explains how the global famine warning *system* is
supposed to work, then uses one displaced-persons camp (Zamzam) as the case study where
it failed. Diagrams the system, then shows the human cost. **Proves:** system-diagram +
case-study structure; graphics as accountability journalism.

### What's driving up burger prices? — ABC News Australia (Silver, 2024)
https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-08-05/cost-of-living-interest-rates-burgers/104156654
National inflation explained through one Big Mac. Rotating food photography, playful
illustration, and charts alternate to keep a dry subject appetizing. **Proves:**
everyday-object anchor, photography/illustration/chart media mixing.

### A Torrent of Trash — Reuters (Bronze, 2024)
https://www.reuters.com/graphics/NORTHKOREA-SOUTHKOREA/TRASH/klvynygmjpg/
North Korea's trash-balloon campaign as an immersive scroll: maps of trajectories,
isometric illustration of payloads, photos of landings. The form (drifting balloons)
literally mirrors the subject. **Proves:** form embodies subject; Reuters' signature
illustrated-immersive format. See also *Buildings wrapped in solid gasoline*
(https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/7037).

## Data art / memorial

### The Birdsong of Sorrow above Ukraine — Anastasia Balagurova (Gold, 2024)
https://birdsong-of-sorrow-above-ukraine.github.io/
Programmatically generated memorial where every civilian death is an individual mark
rendered as birds/song above a landscape. Built explicitly to counter "compassion
fade" — the documented empathy collapse when victims become statistics. Dark, quiet,
slow. **Proves:** generative art as legitimate dataviz; palette/motion restraint as
respect; every-number-is-a-life.

### Aguayos: Migratory Flows within Latin America — (Community winner, 2024)
https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/7588
Migration data woven into the visual language of Andean aguayo textiles — the craft
tradition of the very people the data describes. **Proves:** borrow visual language
from the data's own culture, not from chart libraries.

## Explorable explainers

### Zoonotic Web — Liuhuaying Yang / Complexity Science Hub (Gold, 2024)
https://vis.csh.ac.at/zoonotic-web/
Companion to a Nature Communications paper. A guided scrollytelling tour teaches you to
read a complex network of animal→human disease transmission, *then* hands you the full
interactive dashboard to explore. **Proves:** the guided-tour-then-sandbox pattern —
never drop readers into an unexplained explorable.

### I Want a Better Catastrophe — Jona Pomerance / FH Potsdam (Gold, 2024)
https://flowchart.bettercatastrophe.com/
A book about climate anxiety turned into a walkable branching flowchart with audio
narration. The reader's own choices are the interaction; dread becomes agency.
**Proves:** the flowchart as narrative structure; interaction as emotional argument;
ending heavy topics with agency.

### GOV | DNA — Werner Helmich (WDVP Interactive Winner, 2019)
https://govdna.frontwise.com/
Each country's development indicators rendered as a strand of "DNA" beads; align and
compare strands to see what good government is made of. **Proves:** glyph design — one
composite mark per entity that rewards both squinting (pattern) and reading (detail).

### An Alternative, Data-Driven Country Map — Interacta (WDVP Grand Prize, 2019)
https://projects.interacta.io/country-tsne/
Threw away geography: t-SNE clusters countries by statistical similarity (happiness,
health spend, education), producing a new world map where unexpected neighbors touch.
**Proves:** a novel derived layout can *be* the entire story; algorithmic novelty wins
when it reveals real relationships.

### Good Governments Help People Succeed — Jim Vallandingham (WDVP Runner-up, 2019)
http://vallandingham.me/world_gov_challenge/
Sober analytical walkthrough using small multiples and consistent scales — proof that
restraint and analytical honesty also place. **Proves:** small multiples for many-entity
comparison; you don't need spectacle if the analysis is sharp.

## Static / print / poster

### Swiss Mountains — Fabian Lang (Gold, 2024)
https://www.fabianlang.net/schweizer-bergwelten/
200-page illustrated data book on alpine ecosystems: GPS-tracked vultures, glacier
timelines, 30,000 wildlife sightings. Photography, illustration, and charts unified by
three years of research with domain experts. **Proves:** data + illustration + expert
rigor as one continuous visual language; long-form print pacing.

### Small States can be Big Players — Dimiter Toshkov (WDVP Static Winner, 2019)
https://infobeautiful4.s3.amazonaws.com/2019/02/static_winner.png
Dense single-image infographic correlating government effectiveness with development
outcomes — many panels, one argument, careful reading order. **Proves:** a static can
hold a full argument if panels are sequenced like paragraphs.

### The Value Proposition of Good Government — Anna Jacobson (WDVP Runner-up, 2019)
https://infobeautiful4.s3.amazonaws.com/2019/02/static_runnerup.png
Normalized government performance by national wealth to reveal who does the most with
the least. **Proves:** the derived metric as the creative act — the chart is ordinary,
the lens is new.

### Napkin category winners (WDVP 2019)
https://infobeautiful4.s3.amazonaws.com/2019/02/napkin_winner.png
Hand-drawn entries (Eileen Huang's correlation chart; Rasagy Sharma's "Worst of the
Best, Best of the Worst") placed alongside professional studio work. **Proves:** idea
quality dominates production polish; sketch the idea before investing in craft.

## Environment / place

### Seeking Shadow: A Cool Fix for Hot Cities — Urbi Atlas (Silver, 2024)
https://atlas.urbi.ae/seeking-shadow/
Urban heat explained through shade: digital-twin simulations of shadow paths blended
with hand illustration and historical design references. **Proves:** simulation as data
source; science + illustration blending; a hopeful, solution-shaped ending.

## Sport (closest precedents for youth-sports work)

### Vertical Momentum: High Jump & Pole Vault — Krisztina Szűcs (Silver, 2024)
https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/7194
Athletic performance rendered as elegant animated arcs — the physics of the jump *is*
the chart form. **Proves:** in sport, motion data wants to be drawn as motion.

### Games of two eras — South China Morning Post (Bronze, 2024)
https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/7166
Olympic games across two eras as a direct visual comparison. **Proves:** then-vs-now
framing; side-by-side composition for era comparison.
