About the numbers

Every statistic in this deck comes from published research or national polls, compiled and source-checked before publication. One team of 200 dots carries the whole story: colored dots are athletes, gray dots are non-athletes, and the same dots regroup for every stat. They walk off the field for the 50% dropout, split to scale for the ratio beats (3×, 8×, 92%, 80%), and line up into the bar charts, where each bar is a track of dots filled to its rate. Read the findings honestly. Almost all are correlational: kids who play tend to have these outcomes, but the studies don't prove sports alone cause them. Direction matters too: separately, 77% of adults 30+ who still play sports were sports kids themselves (Robert Wood Johnson Foundation / Harvard / NPR, 2015), but that only describes who today's adult players used to be, not how many of today's kids will stay in the game. Figures marked WSF / Project Play come from organizations that advocate for youth sports, while the University of Kansas, JAACAP, and Eagle Hill figures are independent. Some numbers date from 2004 to 2015. Two widely repeated stats, the "95% of Fortune 500 executives played sports" and "96% of dropouts were non-athletes" lines, are left out because they can't be traced to a primary study.
